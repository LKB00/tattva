Foundations/How AI is marked

AI visual identity and icons

How to show that a reply comes from an assistant.

How Tattva shows that something is AI, what the AI is doing, and which icons to use. The rules are short because we found no standard to copy.

How products show AI

Products use a few common signals to show AI. Each has a benefit and a cost.

Sparkle

For. People know it means AI. Google made it standard in 2023, and there were about 100 versions by 2024.

Against. It cannot say what kind of AI it is. Its meaning depends on where it sits and what the label says. Used alone everywhere, it loses its force.

Gradient and glow

For. Carbon uses light to show AI content. It keeps the glow small and at the edges of a box so text stays readable.

Against. A purple or indigo AI gradient looks generic. Copilot moved from a plain blue hexagon to a rainbow ribbon in 2023. Carbon says not to use it as decoration.

Dedicated hue

For. A color can stand for a brand. Claude uses a warm rust-orange with a starburst. OpenAI kept a blossom mark.

Against. Carbon chose a gradient plus a label instead of one color. Both brand examples come from search snippets.

Avatar

For. Shape of AI calls simple marks low risk. They do not suggest the AI can act on its own.

Against. Branded characters add warmth but can promise too much. Lifelike faces can mislead people.

Orb

For. It shows what the AI is doing with no text, and nothing on the page moves. Apple's iOS 26 Siri is a dark orb that pulses while it listens.

Against. It can feel like a sci-fi cliché. On its own, it does not work for people who cannot see it.

The risk of cliché

The sparkle, the purple gradient and the sci-fi orb each look generic. People know the sparkle means AI. But they cared more about whether the answer is right than which AI wrote it. So the mark says where AI is, and the text says what happened.

Our rules for keeping it quiet

AI

Lime means AI. It is only used as a fill behind charcoal text, never as text on paper.

Needs your approval

Amber means a person has to act, and the words say why. It never marks AI.

Plain surface

No decorative gradients. Any glow is faint, stays at the edge and is never the only signal.

  • One mark for AI across the product. Add text when the action is specific.
  • No purple or rainbow gradients.
  • Every state is also written in words. Color, motion and shape are never the only signal.
  • Controls are pill-shaped. Borders are thin 1px lines.

Labeling AI content

Carbon's AI label can cover anything from one word to a whole page. It opens a small popup with an overview, details, resources and actions. Our AILabel works the same way. The label explains. It never starts an AI action. Once a person edits the content, the tint goes away and an icon button lets them go back to the AI version.

Sizes

Match the label to the text beside it: 16px for 12px text, 18px for 14px, 22px for 16px. Next to icon buttons with no outline, go one size smaller.

What the AI is doing

The presence orb moves through idle, listening, thinking, speaking and error. We did not build a connecting state. One animation runs and only its settings change, so switching states never restarts it. The orb is not a button. A labeled Stop button sits beside it.

Rules

  • Screen readers hear the state text only when the state changes, never on every frame.
  • A visible label shows the same text, unless the state is already written nearby.
  • With reduced motion, the orb stays still, nothing animates, sound level is ignored and changes happen at once.
  • Ask for microphone access only when someone presses a button.
  • Smooth out the raw microphone level over several frames. AIPresence does this for the level prop.

Icon rules

16px
20px
24px
32px
  • Sizes are 16, 20, 24 and 32. Carbon draws on those sizes. 16 and 20 go with 14px and 16px text.
  • Draw on a 24px grid with a 1.5px line and rounded ends and corners. Atlassian's new set also uses 1.5px lines with rounded shapes.
  • Outline is the default. Use a filled version to show something is selected, as Material Symbols does with its FILL setting. We have few filled icons so far.
  • Name an icon for its meaning when it has one purpose. Name it for how it looks when it serves several. This follows Atlassian's two groups. Names end in Icon, such as RegenerateIcon.
  • Use currentColor so the icon matches its text. Do not set a fixed color.
  • Icons are hidden from screen readers. The button holding the icon provides the name. An icon on its own that carries meaning needs a label. A purely decorative one gets an empty label.
  • An icon on its own needs 3:1 contrast (WCAG 1.4.11).
  • Icons you can press need a target of at least 24 by 24 CSS px (WCAG 2.5.8). Carbon asks for 44px, so use 44px on touch screens.
  • Use two stars for Suggest, a pencil with a sparkle for Edit, text lines with a sparkle for Summarize and looping arrows for Remix. These are the habits Shape of AI lists. There is no standard.

What we could not verify

  • We could not get the SF Symbols and Lucide specifications.
  • No source gave dark mode advice or timings for presence orbs, so ours are our own guesses.
  • No source gave advice for high-contrast system modes. We use currentColor and keep a border on tinted labels. We have not checked this.
  • The Copilot, Anthropic, OpenAI and Apple items come from search snippets or an outside blog, so treat them with care.
  • Carbon does not cover chat messages or images, so those label spots are our own guesses.
  • We have not tested the AI mark or its placements with real users.

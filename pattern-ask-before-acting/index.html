Patterns/Ask before acting

Ask before acting

The assistant wants to do something that cannot be undone. What now?

Stop and ask first. Say exactly what will happen, then wait for a clear yes or no.

Try it

Refund an order

high risk
Send $48.00 back to Dana Whitfield for order #1042. This cannot be undone.

Clear out the sale items

Awaiting approval
Nothing has changed yetAbout 2 minutes
  1. 1

    Find items marked as sale

    14 items match.

  2. 2

    Put them back to full price

    Prices change on the live shop.

How to design it

  1. 1

    Sort actions by risk

    Looking things up needs no question. Sending money, deleting or messaging someone does.

  2. 2

    Say what will happen

    Show the real names, amounts and places. Never write "this action".

  3. 3

    Make No as easy as Yes

    Both buttons get the same weight. Saying no must never lead to a lecture.

  4. 4

    Show the result

    After the choice, say what was done or that nothing was done.

Do and avoid

Do

  • Name the person, the amount and the place.
  • Show a plan first when there are many steps.
  • Mark risky actions clearly with amber, because a person has to act.
  • Remember a choice only if the person asks you to.

Avoid

  • Do not ask about every small, safe step.
  • Do not hide the real action behind vague words.
  • Do not pre-select Yes.
  • Do not act first and report later.

Why it works

People accept help more easily when they know they can stop it. A short, clear question at the right moment builds trust. Too many questions teach people to click Yes without reading, so ask only when it matters.

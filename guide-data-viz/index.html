Foundations/Charts guide

Data visualization

How to show numbers in charts people can read.

Tattva charts are drawn in SVG with no chart library. You can read them without color and without a mouse. Every chart comes with a written summary and a table view. This page covers how to pick a chart, the colors, the sizes, the accessibility rules and ready-made dashboards for tracking an AI assistant.

Choosing a chart

Start with the question the reader has. Polaris advises that each chart should answer one specific question. The list below is our own proposal built on that idea.

QuestionUseComponent
How is one measure changing over time?Line chart. Solid for real data, dotted for forecasts.
How do categories compare?Bars that start at zero. Highlight one bar when it is the point.
How is a whole split into parts?Stacked bar with up to four parts. No pie or donut charts.
What is the number now, and how did it move?A tile showing the change, not a chart.
What is the trend in a tiny space?Sparkline with the last point highlighted.
How sure are we about this estimate?A dot with a range line, and a caption that names the range.
Which exact values are there?A table.

Mailchimp and GitLab Pajamas discourage pie charts. Mailchimp recommends a percent bar. Tattva follows that and has no pie or donut. The UK Analysis Function says to use many lines on one chart only when you must, and to prefer a row of small charts instead. So a line chart stops at five lines.

  • The middle color is a neutral and is meant to read as nothing. It is 1.3:1 on white, so cells that use it need a label or an outline.
  • Status colors stay separate from chart colors. Amber is never a series color.

Color rules

  • Series colors go in a fixed order and never repeat. The first series is always chart-1.
  • Four series is the norm and five is the most. Past that, group the rest into one Other series in the neutral, or use a row of small charts. The UK Analysis Function allows at most 4 category colors, or 6 if essential. Atlassian allows 5 to 6. Datawrapper says to avoid more than 7.
  • A color belongs to its series, not to its rank. Hiding a series does not recolor the others.
  • Low-to-high ramps use 3 to 5 steps plus neutral gray for missing data, as the UK Analysis Function advises.
  • Datawrapper's brand tips apply. Use the brand color for a single series. Or use it for the one highlighted category and neutrals for the rest. Stay in one color family. Soften or darken a logo color for large areas.
  • Lime is a highlight in the interface and is never a chart color.
  • Amber means a person must act. It appears only on a test result or number that needs review, and always with text.
  • Do not rely on red versus green alone. Blue with orange or red is safe. Vary lightness too. Use gray for missing or background data.
  • Text is never in the series color. Labels, values and legends use text tokens. A colored mark beside them shows which series it is.

Sizes and parts

These numbers are our own proposals, not checked against a published standard. The UK Analysis Function checklist backs the parts that name it.

PartRule
Lines1.5 to 2px thick, with rounded ends and corners.
BarsStart at zero and are never cut short (UK checklist). 4px rounded at the data end, square at the baseline. 24px thick at most. A 2px gap between neighbors.
Point markersAt least 8px, with a 2px ring in the background color.
GridlinesThin 0.5px lines in --chart-grid, solid and faint. The UK checklist says at most 10 light gray gridlines.
Up-and-down scale labels4 to 6 labels, right-aligned, all text horizontal, with commas in big numbers (UK checklist).
AxesDrawn in --chart-axis. Labels for continuous values sit on the tick marks. Labels for categories sit between them (UK checklist).
TextGeist 12px scale labels with equal-width digits. The display style is for chart titles only.
LegendShow it for two or more series, not for one. Keep the same order as the chart (UK checklist). From the third series, dash and marker shape repeat the color.
Direct labelsAt the end of each line, for up to four series. If labels would overlap, they are dropped and the legend and popup name the series.
Number scalesOne scale only. Never two. Two measures on different scales get two charts.
Minimum sizeAbout 280 by 160. Below that, the chart becomes a sparkline or a table.

Carbon's chart parts are title, axes, tick marks, axis title, legend, toolbar, zoom bar, graph frame and popup. It says the legend should explain each visual property. It has nothing on empty, loading or error states, minimum sizes or small screens, so those rules are ours.

Accessibility rules

Every chart here meets all of these.

  • Text version. A visible summary in body text states the message, and a View as table button shows a real table. The UK Analysis Function checklist requires a data table, a description or both, in body text and not only in alt text. It also recommends a data file people can download. ChartFrame supports this through onDownload.
  • Keyboard. The chart can take focus, and arrow keys step through the points. A screen reader reads each point as series, x value, value and unit. The Highcharts accessibility module offers similar features: keyboard use, screen reader roles, a chart description and a table view.
  • Nothing only on hover. Popups open on focus, pointer and tap. Everything a popup shows is also in the table. Labels on the chart and text columns carry the key values.
  • Reduced motion. When motion is reduced, charts do not animate in and updates happen at once. This is general practice. Highcharts is the only source in our research that covers it.
  • Contrast. WCAG 1.4.11 asks for 3:1 for chart parts people need to understand the content. Parts whose information is also in visible text labels or values are exempt. Category marks pass 3:1. Neutral background marks do not on white, so their values are labeled. The UK guidance asks for 3:1 between neighboring colors and 4.5:1 for chart text, or 3:1 if large.
  • Color is never the only clue. Series 3 and up add dash patterns and marker shapes (circle, square, triangle, diamond). Dashes can look like forecasts, so forecasts are dotted and the dash patterns are kept different. Neighboring fills get a 2px gap, and a white border between sections is a recommended help.

Honest charts and uncertainty

  • Bars start at zero. Charts that show position, such as dot plots, may start elsewhere. The scale shows where.
  • One scale per chart. Two scales invent relationships, so two measures become two charts.
  • Say what a range means by naming the quantity and the level, for example 95% confidence intervals. A confidence interval is the range the true value probably falls in.
  • Ranges that overlap do not reliably mean there is no difference. Range displays can also mislead about how big an effect is (Hofman, Goldstein and Hullman). Captions describe what is drawn. They do not tell readers how to judge significance.
  • In a study with clinicians, a quantile dot plot led to fewer misreadings. Showing the full spread of results shows how much they vary (Wilke). DotPlot draws an estimate and a range. We have not built a full spread view yet.
  • For test scores based on few cases, show a range or the number of cases. This is our own inference. No source covered how to show uncertainty in an AI product.
  • Mark values that may still change. A latest point still arriving is hollow. Forecasts are dotted.

Dashboards and key numbers

Vendor blogs on watching how an AI assistant performs describe four dashboard panels: the slowest response times, how many words are produced, spend per model and error rate. They name time to the first word as a key speed measure. They also list typical and slowest response times by model with call count (p50, p90, p95 and p99 are the percentile names). They add quality measures such as test pass rate, whether answers stick to sources, and user feedback. These are secondary sources. Carbon's dashboard guidance puts the most important data first, so the row of tiles leads and the charts follow.

  • Lower is better for response time, time to the first word, cost and error rate. Set goodDirection to down on those tiles.
  • Words produced is neutral. More is neither good nor bad, so the tile does not say better or worse.
  • Test pass rate shows its range and number of cases in the detail line.
  • One row of filters sits above the tiles and charts. Every number below it covers the same slice of data.

What we could not verify

  • We could not reach the FT Visual Vocabulary, Tufte, Polaris Viz or Material charts pages, so nothing here rests on them.
  • Carbon's chart types page lists 20 or more chart types and gives no rules for choosing.
  • We read the Atlassian color page only as a search snippet, so it is partly unchecked.
  • Our own numbers are unchecked design proposals. They are 4 to 6 scale labels, 0.5px gridlines, 1.5 to 2px lines, markers of at least 8px, and a smallest chart of about 280 by 160 that becomes a sparkline or table.
  • Reduced motion is general practice. Only Highcharts documented it in our research.
  • We found no uncertainty display made for AI products. Showing a range or number of cases for test scores based on few cases is our own inference.
  • The stat tile, change, sparkline and threshold rules have no main source. The lists of AI performance measures come from vendor blogs.
  • We have not tested the charts with screen reader users. The keyboard and screen reader design follows the research but has not been tested with people.

When to use it

Use it for

  • A mini app, like a budget calculator, that our approved parts cannot build.
  • A page from another site that should only show things.
  • A page that may ask to open a link or run a tool, with the person's approval.

Not for

  • A screen our approved parts can draw: use GenUIRenderer
  • Asking for passwords or payment details

Do and avoid

Do

  • Use it only when our approved parts cannot do the job.
  • Declare exactly what the page may ask for, and keep the list short.
  • Keep the sandbox without allow-same-origin.
  • Show who made the page and what it can do, next to the frame.

Avoid

  • Don't build the page's HTML into our own page. Never use dangerouslySetInnerHTML for it.
  • Don't let the page open links or take steps without asking, even for permissions it declared.
  • Don't trust a message just because it says it is from the frame. Check who sent it and from where.
  • Don't ask for passwords or payment details inside a generated page.

On a phone

  • The frame fills the width, and the page inside decides its own layout, so build it for a narrow screen.
  • The frame height follows the page up to the maximum, 640px by default, and scrolls inside after that.
  • The permission buttons wrap under the question. They keep their size and have a 44px tap area on a touch screen.

Seen in products

  • MCP Apps: the app runs in a sandboxed iframe, talks to the host only through messages, declares permissions and can be asked for consent before it calls a tool.
  • MCP Apps: the host can turn off the app's ability to open links.
  • Claude Artifacts: generated pages run in a sandboxed frame on a separate origin.

States

States this part has beyond its default look. Build each one your screen can reach. States set by a property are in the table above.

permission request
When the page asks to open a link or call a tool you allowed, a prompt with Allow and Don't allow appears below the frame.
no permissions
With no permissions, the note says the page can only show things on screen.
resizing
The frame grows or shrinks to the height the page reports, between minHeight and maxHeight.
focus
The frame shows a focus ring on keyboard focus.

Tokens

Change one of these and every part that uses it follows.

  • --surface
  • --border
  • --attention-soft
  • --attention-fg
  • --radius-card

Built in

  • The frame has a name, so screen readers say what it holds.
  • Who made it, what it asked for and what it can do are plain text next to the frame.
  • A permission question moves focus to Allow, with Don't allow next to it.
  • A clear outline shows when someone tabs into the frame.
  • The frame grows to fit the page but stays between a smallest and a largest height.

What you need to do

  1. 1Give it a title that says what the page holds. Screen readers use it to name the frame.
  2. 2Check the page inside for accessibility yourself. The frame cannot fix it.
  3. 3Prefer GenUIRenderer when it can do the job, because its parts are already accessible.

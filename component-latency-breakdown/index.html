LatencyBreakdown

StableNewPro, locked. The preview is free, the code comes with Pro.

Shows where the time went in a voice reply or a whole call, names the slowest stage in words and lists the turns that were too slow.

When to use it

Use it for

  • The detail page of one call in an agent dashboard.
  • Checking one reply that felt slow while testing an agent.
  • Comparing a change to the model or voice against your team's target.

Not for

  • Showing people on the call that the assistant is working: use VoicePanel
  • A single number on a dashboard: use StatTile
  • Response times over days or weeks: use LineChart

All guidance, with do and avoid and what it does on a phone.

When to use it

Helps the people who run a voice agent see why it felt slow, and which part to fix first, without reading a chart.

Made of: Heading and target · Total · Percentiles · Slowest stage line · Stacked bar · Stage table · Slow turns list

Use it for

  • The detail page of one call in an agent dashboard.
  • Checking one reply that felt slow while testing an agent.
  • Comparing a change to the model or voice against your team's target.

Not for

  • Showing people on the call that the assistant is working: use VoicePanel.
  • A single number on a dashboard: use StatTile.
  • Response times over days or weeks: use LineChart.

Do and avoid

Do

  • Name the slowest stage. A total alone does not say what to fix.
  • Measure time to the first real answer, so a filler word cannot hide a slow reply.
  • Link slow turns to the recording, so people can hear what happened.

Avoid

  • Do not colour stages lime. Lime marks the AI, and this is about time.
  • Do not use amber for a slow turn. Nobody has to act on one call.
  • Do not show the target as a law. It is a goal your team set.

On a phone

  • On a phone the stacked bar is hidden and each table row shows its own small bar, so the split reads as a list.
  • Slow turns are full-width buttons with a 44px tap area on touch screens.
  • Padding shrinks a step below 640px wide so the numbers keep their room.

Seen in products

  • Retell shows end-to-end P50, P90 and P99 per call, split into speech recognition, model, voice, knowledge base and speech-to-speech.
  • Vapi shows turn count, average turn latency and a split by transcriber, endpointing, model, voice and transport, with a table per turn.
  • Twig suggests tracking time to the first real answer, so filler words cannot hide a slow reply.
  • Retell: Check actual latency (opens in a new tab)
  • Vapi: Call logs (opens in a new tab)
  • Twig: the 800 ms rule of thumb (opens in a new tab)

States

States this part has beyond its default look. Build each one your screen can reach. States set by a property are in the table above.

within target
The total is at or under the team's target: the total reads in ink with the words Within target.
over target
The total is over the target: it says Over target by and the number of milliseconds, in words, never by colour alone.
slow turns
In call mode, turns over the target are listed as buttons; pressing one calls onTurnSelect so the app can jump to that moment.
no slow turns
In call mode with every turn inside the target, a plain line says so instead of an empty list.
phone
Under 640px the stacked bar is hidden and each row of the table gets its own small bar, so nothing is squeezed.

Built in

  • The stacked bar is hidden from screen readers. A real table follows with every stage, its time in ms and its share.
  • The slowest stage is written in a sentence and marked as slowest in the table, not only shown by colour.
  • Over and within target are written in words.
  • Slow turns are buttons with their name, time and slowest stage in the label.
  • The bar grows in once and stops; under reduced motion it is drawn still.

What you need to do

  1. 1Pass times in milliseconds that your code measured. Do not round them into words yourself.
  2. 2If you pass onTurnSelect, make it move your audio player and say where it moved, as the example does.
  3. 3Set targetMs from your team's own goal and call it that. Do not present it as an industry rule.

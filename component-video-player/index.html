Components/Create/VideoPlayer

VideoPlayer

Stable

Plays a generated clip and lets you act on the exact frame you paused on.

Also known as video, media player

Install with npm install @tattva/ui (setup), or copy the code from the kit in Downloads.

Generated take with frame actions

Take 3, harbour at dawn

8 seconds, 24 frames a second

0:00 / 0:08 · frame 1
Paused at 0:00, frame 1

[Gulls and soft waves]

Narrator: The first boat leaves just before sunrise.

Shows a small sailing boat crossing a calm harbour at dawn, from left to right, past a striped lighthouse.

These work while focus is inside the player, and never while typing.

  • SpaceK Play or pause
  • ←→ Back or forward 1 second
  • ,. or Shift with an arrow: one frame
  • HomeEnd Start or end
  • M Mute
  • C Captions

<VideoPlayer
  label="Take 3, harbour at dawn"
  subtitle="8 seconds, 24 frames a second"
  duration={8}
  fps={24}
  aiGenerated
  provenance={{
    madeWith: "Video model, draft quality",
    date: "3 October 2026",
    editedByPerson: false,
    actions: ["Made from your prompt and one start frame"],
    why: "The file carries an invisible watermark and Content Credentials. Both can be lost if the clip is re-uploaded or re-encoded.",
  }}
  cues={cues}
  transcript={transcript}
  frameActions={[
    { id: "start", label: "Use as start frame" },
    { id: "extend", label: "Extend from here" },
    { id: "save", label: "Save frame" },
  ]}
  onFrameAction={(id, seconds, frame) => useFrame(id, seconds, frame)}
/>

// With a real file, the length comes from the file:
<VideoPlayer label="Take 3, harbour at dawn" src="/takes/3.mp4" poster="/takes/3.jpg" fps={24}
  captions={[{ src: "/takes/3.vtt", lang: "en", label: "English" }]} />

No file is needed: the player draws a stand-in scene and simulates time. Press Play, pause anywhere, then step frames with the buttons, or with comma and period while focus is in the player. The paused-frame actions return the time and frame number.

UsageCodeDesignAccessibility

When to use it

Lets someone watch a generated take, stop on the exact frame they want and use it, with every state in words.

Made of: Title · AI label or provenance · State or subtitle · Frame (16:9, 9:16 or 1:1) · Working edge while generating · Captions · Scrubber · Play · Frame back and forward · Time and frame · Loop · Mute · Captions toggle · Full screen · Paused-frame actions · Transcript · Keyboard shortcuts

Use it for

  • Reviewing a take the AI made, then using a frame as the start of the next shot.
  • Picking the point to extend or edit from.
  • Showing a clip with captions and a transcript as its text alternative.

Not for

  • A still image the AI made: use MediaFrame.
  • Audio only, such as a call or a voice: use AudioPlayer.
  • Choosing a stretch of the clip to edit or extend: use ClipRangeSelector.
  • Progress of a long job with steps: use GenerationProgress.

Do and avoid

Do

  • Wait for a press before playing anything.
  • Offer frame actions that match the next step, such as Use as start frame or Extend from here.
  • Label every AI clip and say what the file carries.
  • Give a transcript or captions for clips with speech.

Avoid

  • Don't autoplay, or play on hover under reduced motion.
  • Don't colour the player, the scrubber or the progress with the AI colour. Only the label and the working edge use it.
  • Don't show a countdown or a made-up percentage while generating.
  • Don't use red or blame words for a blocked take. Say what was blocked and whether credits came back.

On a phone

  • The Play button is 44px; every other control grows to 44px on touch screens. The controls wrap on narrow screens.
  • The scrubber's hit area is 44px tall even though the track is 4px.
  • Tap the frame to play or pause. Double-tap its left or right side to go back or forward 2 seconds.
  • Paused-frame actions sit in a row under the controls and wrap to full width, with 44px buttons.
  • A 9:16 clip is capped in width so it does not push the controls off a phone screen.

Seen in products

  • Google Flow: Shift with an arrow pauses and steps one frame.
  • Sora's Loop editor had two handles for the start and end of the loop.
  • The best players let you pause on a frame and save it, extend from there or edit from there.
  • Flow labels every output with an invisible SynthID watermark; the visible watermark is a toggle.

States

States this part has beyond its default look. Build each one your screen can reach. States set by a property are in the Code tab.

generating
state="generating": first frame or poster dimmed under a working edge; nothing can play.
loading
state="loading", or while a src loads: a spinner and disabled controls.
paused on a frame
Pausing shows the frame actions with the time and frame number.
error
state="error" or a file that fails: a calm message, statusNote and Try again.
one at a time
Starting any video or audio player pauses the others on the page.

Built in

  • The player is a region named by its title.
  • Play is a toggle button named Play with aria-pressed. When it cannot play it uses aria-disabled and stays focusable.
  • The scrubber is a slider with the time in words, such as "3.2 seconds of 8 seconds, frame 77 of 192".
  • The frame step buttons say the frame they go to, such as "Forward one frame, to frame 78".
  • Loop, Mute and Captions are toggle buttons with aria-pressed. A clip with no sound says "No sound in this clip".
  • Space plays or pauses while focus is in the player, except on a button. Arrows seek 1 second, Shift with an arrow steps a frame, Home and End jump to the ends.
  • K, M, C, comma and period work only while focus is in the player and never in a text field. A switch in Keyboard shortcuts turns them off.
  • The paused-frame actions are a named group that says the frame number.
  • Ready after generating, ended, buffering and error are announced once. The clock and play or pause are not.
  • Captions use native tracks for files, and the transcript sits in a collapsible section.
  • The big play mark on the frame is for pointers only and is hidden from screen readers; the Play button is the real control.

What you need to do

  1. 1Give a label that tells takes apart, such as "Take 3, harbour at dawn". It names the player and its scrubber.
  2. 2Give a transcript, or cues for captions, for any clip with speech. Describe what the clip shows there too: the prompt is not a description.
  3. 3Never start playback from code. There is no autoplay prop on purpose.
  4. 4Say what the file carries in provenance (watermark, Content Credentials) and what can strip it. Do not promise more.
  5. 5Pass statusNote for a failed or blocked take that says whether credits came back.

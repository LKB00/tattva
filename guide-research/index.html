Guides/Project/Research

AI product patterns

What we learned from people using assistants.

Before growing the system, we looked at AI products that are already out and at published guidelines. Then we compared them with what Tattva had. This page lists what we found, how well each finding is backed up, and where each pattern lives in the library.

Method and limits

  • We looked at four areas at once: chat assistants, tools where AI does tasks for you and tools for developers, tools that help inside your own work or make images and media, and published guidelines and pattern libraries.
  • Each pattern is tagged Primary (company docs and official posts), Secondary (news, community threads or search summaries) or Inference (no source found, so we reasoned it out).
  • Some pages could not be read. OpenAI and Perplexity help pages gave access errors, and Apple's guidelines only load with scripts. What we know about those products comes from secondary sources.
  • Products change fast. Treat these notes as a snapshot from October 2026.

30 of the 35 patterns below were added this time. The rest were already in the library.

Principles we applied

  • Calibrate trust

    People should know when to rely on the AI and when to use their own judgment (PAIR). Tattva helps with sources, confidence labels, popups that show where an answer came from, and plain notices.

  • Prefer partial explanations

    PAIR suggests short, partial explanations over complete ones. The popup that shows where an answer came from works in layers: a label first, details when asked.

  • Mark every instance of AI

    Carbon asks for AI labels everywhere, styled to be useful. The assistant avatar, AIBadge and the source popup show the label as text, not color alone.

  • Make errors recoverable

    Microsoft guidelines 7 to 11 ask for easy ways to start, dismiss and correct the AI, to do less when unsure, and to explain why. Retry, Discard, Revert and Rewind exist for this.

  • Show what really happened

    Anthropic advises showing plans, showing the result of each step, and adding points where a person checks in and conditions that stop the work. PlanCard, ToolCallCard, PermissionPrompt and CheckpointList follow that.

  • Disclose consequences and cost

    Cost estimates, credits and retries that count toward a usage limit are shown before the person commits.

Accessibility finding applied

Guidance for accessible AI chat says not to mark the text that is still arriving as a live region. A screen reader would read out every small change. Message now turns off live announcements while text is arriving. It announces one short status when the reply is done. The same guidance asks that the cursor stays in the message box after sending, and that the Stop button can still be reached by keyboard while the reply is written. Composer already does both.

Not built yet

  • Browser-agent live view with take-over

    Reported for ChatGPT agent and Claude in Chrome, but our sources were search snippets and extension docs. It needs a real live-view screen, so design it with a product team.

  • Background task chip and needs-input inbox

    Partly covered by AgentSessionList. Notifications and browser tab titles belong to the app that uses the library.

  • Group chats and persona pickers

    Released by ChatGPT, Copilot, Grok and Character.ai. Our sources were mostly secondary, and the patterns are specific to each product.

  • Brush and mask editor, editing one part of an image

    Seen in ChatGPT image editing, Photoshop and Figma Make. These need a drawing engine more than a component.

  • Compare slider, audio and video players

    We found no primary source this time.

  • Workflow canvas (boxes joined by lines)

    Vercel AI Elements has one. It is a separate kind of screen, so it is out of scope for now.

  • Apple, Salesforce, Atlassian, Adobe, Material and Fluent guidance

    Pages could not be opened, or we did not look at them. They are still unreviewed.

When to use it

Use it for

  • Live captions under a voice assistant.
  • A support call transcript where the assistant and the caller are both shown.
  • A recorded call where each line jumps the audio to that point.

Not for

  • A typed chat thread: use MessageList
  • A stream of agent steps or logs: use LiveLog
  • The whole voice screen with orb and controls: use VoicePanel

All guidance, with do and avoid and what it does on a phone.

When to use it

Makes a voice conversation readable without sound, and honest about what was really said. It is the accessible version of voice.

Made of: Header with label · Size control · Hide or Show button · Caption log · Speaker label (AI mark for the assistant) · Time · State word · Caption text · Jump to latest · Live region (hidden, optional)

Use it for

  • Live captions under a voice assistant.
  • A support call transcript where the assistant and the caller are both shown.
  • A recorded call where each line jumps the audio to that point.

Not for

  • A typed chat thread: use MessageList.
  • A stream of agent steps or logs: use LiveLog.
  • The whole voice screen with orb and controls: use VoicePanel.

Do and avoid

Do

  • Keep the same id when an interim line becomes final, so it settles in place.
  • Pass spokenUpTo from the audio that really played.
  • Name every speaker in words, and list AI speakers in aiSpeakers.
  • Store the size and visible choices for the person.

Avoid

  • Do not colour the person's captions lime. Only the AI label carries the AI wash.
  • Do not live-announce every word. It fights the audio and the screen reader.
  • Do not show speech the assistant never played as if it was said.
  • Do not show emotion scores about the person.

On a phone

  • Caption text starts at the large size on screens narrower than 600px, and medium elsewhere.
  • The header wraps: the size control and the Hide button drop under the label on a narrow screen.
  • Lines wrap and never scroll sideways, so the log works at 320px wide.
  • The Hide and Show button and Jump to latest have 44px touch areas.

Seen in products

  • Gemini Live: a captions toggle that stays on for all Live chats, with size and style settings.
  • Google Meet: caption font, size and colour settings, saved automatically and private to each viewer.
  • OpenAI Realtime API: conversation.item.truncate trims the transcript to the audio that was played.
  • Vapi call logs: transcript lines that seek the recording.
  • 9to5Google, Gemini Live captions (opens in a new tab)
  • Google Meet Help, translated captions (opens in a new tab)
  • OpenAI Community, interruptions do not trim the transcript (opens in a new tab)

States

States this part has beyond its default look. Build each one your screen can reach. States set by a property are in the table above.

empty
Before the first line, the log shows the empty text.

When the person scrolls up, following stops and Jump to latest shows how many captions arrived since.

Built in

  • The log is a focusable role=log region named by its heading, with aria-live off, so people read it on demand with the arrow keys.
  • Each speaker is named in text. AI labels add the AI mark, never colour alone.
  • Interim lines show the words Still changing, interrupted lines say Interrupted, and hidden parts say Part hidden.
  • Unspoken words are struck through and start with Not spoken for screen readers.
  • With announceFinal, a polite status region reads each finished AI line once; interim words are never read.
  • New lines fade in over --dur-fast. Under reduced motion they appear at once.
  • With onSeek, each line is a real button.

What you need to do

  1. 1Store visible and size for the person and pass them back next time, so the setting persists across sessions.
  2. 2Announce the session state (listening, speaking) somewhere else, once, in a status region. The log stays quiet.
  3. 3Turn on announceFinal for people who mute the audio and use a screen reader.
  4. 4Replace secrets with [hidden] before they reach the caption text. The part never sees the secret.
  5. 5Set spokenUpTo from the audio that was really played, so the transcript matches what the person heard.
  6. 6Offer the full transcript after the session ends, and a way to flag wrong words where it becomes a record.

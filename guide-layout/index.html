Foundations/Layout and grid

Layout, grid and hierarchy

How to arrange a screen on phones and desktops.

How a page is divided, how space groups things, and how people know where to look first. Numbers come from published systems where we could check them. Where we could not, we say so.

Screen sizes

Tattva has five screen size classes, with breaks at 600, 840, 1200 and 1600px. They match the Material 3 classes. Tailwind's sm, md, lg and xl use the same widths.

Margin is the empty space at the page edge. The gap between columns is called a gutter. Their values are the --margin and --gutter tokens, which change at 840 and 1200px. Grid switches its columns at md and lg by default (4, 8, 12). That is later than Material. Treat the table as the goal and pass cols to match it. This is a proposal to confirm.

Columns, margins and gaps

Columns split up the content area. The margin sits outside it. The gutter is the gap between columns. Use Grid for columns and Container for margins.

Spacing and grouping

  • Space comes from a scale of eight steps, in multiples of 4px, times the density token. Stack and Cluster accept a gap from 1 to 8.
  • Items that sit close together look like a group. Put less space inside a group than between groups (NN/g proximity).
  • If space alone does not show the groups, add a border or a background box (NN/g visual hierarchy).
  • GOV.UK shrinks its large spacing on phones. Tattva does not do this yet.
  • Stacking on a phone can split a group. Check the order after a layout collapses.

Making one thing stand out

NN/g names three tools: color and contrast, size, and grouping. Use at most 3 sizes, 2 large elements and 3 levels of contrast. Try the squint test: blur your eyes and check that the first thing you see is the right one. Our ranking, strongest to weakest, is size, weight, contrast, position and color. This ranking is our own and has no source.

People scan pages in four ways, named F, spotted, layer-cake and commitment. Layer-cake works best. The F pattern shows up when content is not broken into chunks (NN/g). Use headings to break up long answers.

Chat on smaller screens

The steps below are our own, built on the Material screen sizes. Material also lists standard layouts called list-detail, supporting pane and feed (from a snippet).

Phone keyboards

  • Chrome 108 and later uses interactive-widget=resizes-visual by default. The keyboard covers part of the page but does not resize it, so fixed elements and vh units stay where they were (Chrome).
  • A message box pinned to the bottom therefore needs visualViewport listeners, interactive-widget=resizes-content or the VirtualKeyboard API.
  • We expect dvh to suit the page height, but we have not checked.

Tight and roomy spacing

Add data-density="compact" to any area to change --density from 1 to 0.8. Stack, Cluster and Grid shrink their gaps to match. The 0.8 value is a proposal. The systems we reviewed give no number.

Text size and line length

Three heading sizes grow smoothly with the window. They use clamp with a rem term, because sizes based only on window width can fail WCAG 1.4.4 when people zoom (Smashing). WCAG 1.4.8 asks for lines of at most 80 characters, line spacing of at least 1.5, paragraph spacing of at least 1.5 times the line spacing, and zoom to 200% without sideways scrolling. See also .

Body text is capped at 72 characters per line by the measure token. Short lines are easier to read. WCAG 1.4.8 sets a limit of 80. By our math, the 768px chat width gives about 70 to 75. Resize the window and watch the headings above scale smoothly.

What we could not verify

  • Container widths of 768, 680 and 1120px are proposals to confirm.
  • Material margins and column counts came from search snippets. The screen size widths are checked.
  • Atlassian screen breaks, columns and margins came from a snippet.
  • The density values of 0.75 to 0.8 have no source. Neither does choosing divider lines over empty space. NN/g says to use space first and add a box when that is not enough.
  • We did not check Apple HIG or Refactoring UI rules. The ranking of size, weight and the rest is our own.
  • The split ratios for Claude Artifacts and ChatGPT Canvas come from blog posts.
